ローカルLLM実測

8 月の最大関心＝ローカルで動くオープンモデルの「速く・安く」を、Qwen3.8-Flash-Next（125B MoE・アクティブ 6B）で徹底計測した 2 本。177B のうち 51B を占める n-gram テーブル（PLE）は乗算されず gather されるだけなので VRAM に入れず SSD から mmap で供給——旧 Ampere 2 枚で 131K コンテキスト・54tok/s、量子化を小さく全層 GPU に載せると 5.7 倍速。もう 1 本は同じマシンで vLLM 対 llama.cpp を突き合わせ、「シングルストリームの decode は llama.cpp、TTFT・プレフィル・並列は vLLM」と切り分ける。

@JoelDeTeves 08/30 14:17 JST
原文
Running Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (125B MoE, 6B active) on two old Ampere GPUs at 54+ tok/s with 131K context. RTX 3090 (24GB) + RTX A6000 (48GB) = 72GB VRAM, 192GB DDR4 RAM, and llama.cpp. The key insight: 51B of the model's 177B params are an n-gram lookup table (PLE). It's read once per token at a deterministic address — ~2.7KB per token, ~3MB/s of random reads. That pages from NVMe via mmap and never enters VRAM. The remaining 54.5GB of actual weights fit in 72GB VRAM with room for 131K context. The trick was using @AtomicChat's AD-4.27bpw-Q4_K_M imatrix quant (94.5 GB). They isolate the PLE table in its own GGUF shard so llama.cpp puts it on the host automatically — no --override-tensor needed. Their imatrix calibration is excellent, measured against BF16 reference logits with a public corpus. Serve flags: llama-server -m ...gguf -ngl 999 --tensor-split 2,1 --fit off -lm mmap --tensor-read-lazy on --spec-type ngram-mod -fa on -c 131072 --jinja Results: • Generation: 54 tok/s (all 48 layers on GPU, zero CPU offload) • Prompt processing: 47.5 tok/s • Context: 131,072 tokens • ngram-mod speculation: active (14% draft acceptance) • 3090: 20.7/24.5 GB VRAM, A6000: 39.0/49.1 GB VRAM For comparison: Unsloth UD-Q4_K_XL (111 GB) only fit 38/48 layers on GPU with 10 on CPU → 8.2 tok/s. Getting all layers on GPU with the smaller quant = 5.7x speedup. Quality tradeoff is modest: 92.3% → 89.5% top-1 accuracy (KLD 0.047 → 0.084). This architecture is a preview of Qwen4. 125B params with 6B active per token + a 51B n-gram table that offloads to SSD. A 177B model generating at 54 tok/s on two consumer GPUs from 2020. The future of local inference is architectural innovation, not just bigger GPUs. Same model, same box, two engines. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on vLLM vs llama.cpp on a DGX Spark. New runs are in the atlas, and the recipe repo had a busy week. The split is not "llama.cpp is slower." It's sharper than that. Single stream, llama.cpp wins decode: 54 tok/s per request vs 34 on vLLM. Sixty percent faster where it counts for one person typing at one model.
